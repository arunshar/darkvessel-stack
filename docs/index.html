Abstract

A ship goes dark by silencing, spoofing, or jamming its AIS transponder, the GPS beacon vessels are legally required to broadcast. Catching it means fusing: AIS says where ships claim to be; Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical say where ships actually are. Each sensor has a blind spot, optical is blocked by cloud and night, SAR is grainy, AIS is trivially spoofable, so a vessel cannot hide in every modality at once. DarkVesselNet co-registers SAR onto the optical grid with the real per-pixel sensor models, embeds both through a shared geospatial foundation-model backbone, and scores the contradiction between the imagery and the AIS track. The anomaly half is the author's published thesis work made operational: TGARD scores how kinematically infeasible an AIS gap is, and the Pi-DPM head scores how implausible a reconstructed track is against a bicycle-model prior.

Honest scope. This is the breadth showcase, the modern remote-sensing CV stack a senior scientist is expected to wield. What is real and tested (53 tests pass, verified live) is a toolkit of EO primitives (Lee speckle filter, cloud masking + spectral band ratios, haversine + the TGARD rendezvous algorithm), a foundation-model adapter (GeoBackbone over six real models, with a CPU stub mode), a distortion-aware co-registration warp, and the Pi-DPM anomaly head. The headline "7 canonical EO tasks under one config tree" and the xView3-SAR leaderboard are the roadmap, not seven trained pipelines. The anomaly task is the one with real, published math.

How it works

Detection is the contradiction: AIS says "empty," the imagery shows a vessel. The anomaly head adds a physics-implausibility score.

The rendezvous logic (TGARD), in one paragraph

A legitimate AIS gap (lost signal in a dead zone) still has the vessel moving at its usual speed, so it covers roughly speed·dt. A suspicious gap is one where the endpoints are far closer than the vessel could have traveled, meaning it slowed or stopped, often to meet another vessel at sea. The feasibility score 1 - d/reach (where reach = max_sog·dt) is near 1 when almost none of the reachable envelope was used. This is a kinematic-feasibility test vendored from the author's ACM TIST 2024 paper. The Pi-DPM head (SIGSPATIAL GeoAnomalies 2025) additionally scores how implausible a reconstructed track is against a single-axle Kinematic Bicycle Model, so even a spoofed-smooth track gets flagged. The co-registration reuses the same analytic-Jacobian skill as the author's sat-splat work, the dissertation through-line: Distortion-Aware Spatial Data Science to Sat-Splat-Distort to DarkVesselNet.

Why it matters

Multimodal sensor fusion

SAR + optical + AIS through a swappable foundation-model backbone is the geospatial team's remit. The maritime domain is the vehicle; the methods (co-registration, foundation-model adapters, anomaly detection) carry over to street and road imagery.

Published anomaly contribution

The detection is not a black box: TGARD and Pi-DPM are the author's peer-reviewed algorithms made operational, and every decision flows back through a trace into an analyst-reviewable observation, not just an alert.

Honest next step

Make one task real end to end: vessel detection on the public xView3-SAR sample with a DETR head on Prithvi-2 tokens, then demonstrate the fusion win, a case where AIS says "empty" but SAR + the detector find a vessel. One trained task beats seven scaffolded ones.
